You're flying over Venus

Thick clouds hide Venus's surface from ordinary cameras. NASA's Magellan spacecraft mapped it from orbit with radar between 1990 and 1994, covering about 98% of the planet.

Everything here is that real data. The ground's brightness is Magellan's radar image of the surface, and the hills are its altimeter measurements. You can visit the volcanoes, highland plateaus, impact craters and lava channels of Earth's twin, at their real locations and full planetary scale.

Heights are exaggerated ×3 by default because Venus is surprisingly flat. Change it with the [ and ] keysthe ×3 button. The HUD always shows real altitudes. The radar is ~2 km per pixel, so the view is best from 30–300 km up. The orange haze is artistic: the real surface sits under a crushing CO₂ atmosphere at 465 °C.
